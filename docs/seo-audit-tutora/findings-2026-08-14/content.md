# Content Quality / E-E-A-T Re-audit — tutora.it.com (2026-08-14)

**Baseline:** 66/100 (2026-08-10) — see `docs/seo-audit-tutora/findings/content.md` and `content-depth-audit-2026-08-10.md`.

**New score: 76/100** (+10 vs. baseline)

**Method:** Live fetches via `render_page.py` (`--mode auto`, raw/SSR confirmed on every page) for `/`, `/about`, `/request-a-tutor`, 4 course pages (`python-ff654450`, `portuguese-4702233f`, `ielts-8d4686db`, `arabic-bee72fa4`), 4 subject pages (`gcse-maths`, `spanish`, `algebra`, `ielts`), and 4 tutor profiles (`preeti-parihar`, `abeer-singh`, `aditi-sinha`, `manish`). This was cross-checked against a full static-analysis pass of `lib/course-subject-content.ts` (53 entries) and `lib/subject-content.ts` (103 entries) to get distribution data across the *entire* corpus, not just the live sample, since that's what actually changed since the baseline (git commits: "Add courseDetail/subjectDetail content to remaining 146 course/subject pages", "improved the subject and courses detail page", "improved the detail tutor page", "improved the geo issues", "fixed the request a tutor page").

Note on the `--json` CLI flag: it truncates `content`/`raw_content`/`extracted_text` to ~500 chars for terminal display; this audit used `--output <file>` to capture full HTML and re-ran extraction locally (trafilatura + BeautifulSoup) to get real word counts — the truncated `--json` mode would have under-reported every page's word count by ~80-90%, worth flagging for future skill runs.

## What genuinely improved since 2026-08-10

1. **Course pages (53/53) now have hand-written `courseDetail` prose, up from 6/53 pilot pages at baseline.** Static analysis of `lib/course-subject-content.ts`: differentiation + courseDetail + FAQ prose now averages **556 words/entry** (range 302-698), up from the baseline's 266-word average. Every single course entry has a `courseDetail` field — the code-coverage gap flagged at baseline (only a 6-page pilot existed) is fully closed. Content reads as genuinely specific and non-generic: e.g. the Python entry's `courseDetail` explains indentation-as-syntax, mutable-default-argument gotchas, and traceback-reading — real, checkable technical facts, not filler. Same standard held on manual review of Portuguese, Arabic, and IELTS entries (thinnest in the set, still contain real subject-specific detail: IELTS's four-section 40-question format and band-score criteria; Arabic's diglossia between MSA and spoken dialects).
2. **Subject pages (99/103) now have `subjectDetail` prose and a synthesized "About [Subject] tutoring" paragraph, up from 6/103 + a code-level synthesis fallback at baseline.** Static analysis of `lib/subject-content.ts`: average 458 words/entry (range 52-603). The structural gap flagged at baseline (subject pages had *no* equivalent to `aboutCourseParagraph()`) is now closed site-wide via `aboutSubjectParagraph()`, confirmed present in live HTML on `gcse-maths` and `spanish`.
3. **Live word counts now clear the stated floors on sampled pages.** Full-page text extraction (main content, nav/footer/script stripped) on `/courses/python-ff654450` returned **1,217 words** — well above the 800 floor (this total includes matched-tutor cards and FAQ, which is legitimate on-page content, not padding). `/subjects/gcse-maths` and `/subjects/spanish` both clear their 500-600 floor once the new synthesized paragraph + `subjectDetail` + FAQ are counted.
4. **3 of 103 subject pages remain intentionally thin "chooser" router pages** (`algebra` 52 words, `ap-calculus` 72, `ap-physics` 137) — correct by design (they route to more specific subject pages), not a regression. Confirmed on live fetch of `/subjects/algebra`.
5. **No new duplicate-content risk introduced by the new prose.** The new `courseDetail`/`subjectDetail` paragraphs are per-entry, subject-specific technical content (not a shared template with name substitution), which is the opposite failure mode from the "based in India" closing-clause duplication flagged at baseline.

## What has NOT improved / remains flagged

1. **Tutor bio closing-sentence template is still present, confirmed on more profiles this round.** Live-sampled 4 profiles (up from 1-2 at baseline): `abeer-singh` — "Based in India, he teaches online through TutorA, with every session live and 1:1 — matched to a student's specific subject, level, and curriculum..."; `manish` — "He's based in India and teaches online through TutorA, with every session live and 1:1, matched to a student's specific language and level." Same skeleton, name/subject swapped. `preeti-parihar` and `aditi-sinha` didn't contain the literal phrase "based in India" in this sample (bio wording varies slightly), suggesting *some* admin-level variation exists, but the core "live and 1:1... matched to a student's specific X" clause recurs. This was flagged at baseline as "plausible but unconfirmed at scale" since bios live in Postgres, not code — now independently confirmed on a larger live sample. No code-level fix is possible here (per the 2026-08-10 depth audit, this is a DB/admin-bio-writing problem, not a template problem) — still the single most visible "AI-generated-sounding" pattern left on the site for a human rater to notice.
2. **Course/subject-page richest-vs-thinnest spread is real and still uneven.** Thinnest 10 course entries (Portuguese 302w, Arabic 312w, Business English 366w) sit well below the 556-word average and below the 800 target on hand-written prose alone; richest (Python 698w, SQL 694w, Robotics 651w) comfortably clear it. Thinnest 10 subject entries below the average (Finance Basics 175w, Spanish 190w, Creative Writing 230w, Grammar 231w) — notably `spanish`, one of the highest-intent/highest-traffic subject pages, is one of the thinnest. This mirrors the baseline's finding that language and lower-prestige subjects get systematically less differentiation content; the gap has narrowed in absolute terms (was 107-133w before any work; now 175-248w) but the *relative* pattern — foreign-language and "standard"-template subjects thinnest — persists.
3. **No new E-E-A-T trust signals found.** No author credentials, no external citations/press, no aggregateRating schema, no testimonials — same gaps as baseline. This is a deliberate business choice per the codebase's own comments ("No testimonials section exists anywhere in this codebase — deliberately skipped per business decision"), not an oversight, but it caps Authoritativeness regardless of prose depth.
4. **FAQ heading semantics not independently re-verified this round on `/courses` hub and `/find-a-tutor` hub** (the two templates flagged with defects at baseline — plain `<span>` on `/courses` hub, nested `<h3>` bug on `/find-a-tutor`). Detail-page templates (`/courses/[slug]`, `/subjects/[slug]`, tutor profiles) were confirmed using proper FAQPage schema + heading markup in this round's live samples (`gcse-maths` FAQPage schema confirmed present; 7 `<h3>` tags counted on that page, consistent with clean FAQ-question semantics). **Data gap:** did not re-fetch the two hub pages themselves this round to confirm whether the specific defects noted at baseline were fixed — flagging as unverified rather than assuming either way.
5. **`/request-a-tutor` fetch returned an HTML file but was not independently text-analyzed in depth this round** (git log shows "fixed the request a tutor page" as a recent commit) — captured the page but ran out of scope to do a full word-count/E-E-A-T pass on it before this report was due. Flagging as a data gap for a follow-up pass rather than guessing.
