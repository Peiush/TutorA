# Phase 4 — Outreach Materials (ready to send)

Every template below uses only real, already-established facts about TutorA: live 1:1
online tutoring (not pre-recorded), tutors personally reviewed by the team before being
matched (not an open marketplace), matching-based (not payment-processing) platform,
pricing shown before booking, no certificate of completion, real course pages (53) and
subject pages (103) to link to. **Bracketed placeholders** need your real input (your
name, a real founding date/story detail, a specific stat if you have one) — nothing is
pre-filled with invented numbers, credentials, or outcomes. Fill those in before sending.

Contact used throughout: `tutora.support@gmail.com` (confirm this is still the right
inbox for outreach replies before sending).

## 1. HARO / journalist-request response template

Use on Qwoted, Featured.com (free tier), Help a B2B Writer, or `#JournoRequest` on X.
Journalists get dozens of pitches — lead with the direct answer, keep it under 150 words,
offer a real quote attributed to a real person at TutorA (you, or a tutor who consents).

```
Subject: Re: [exact query title from their request]

Hi [journalist name],

[One direct, quotable answer to their actual question — 2-3 sentences, no fluff, written
as if it could be pasted straight into their article as a quote.]

Quick context: I'm [your name/role] at TutorA (tutora.it.com), an online 1:1 tutoring
marketplace where every tutor is personally reviewed before being matched with a student
— not an open listing board. [One more sentence of real, specific detail relevant to
their query, e.g. what subjects you see the most demand for, or a real observation from
running the platform — no invented statistics.]

Happy to expand on any of this or do a quick call if useful. Available at
tutora.support@gmail.com.

Best,
[Your name]
[Title], TutorA
```

**Good query topics to watch for** (search these terms on HARO/Qwoted daily): "tutoring,"
"back to school," "study tips," "AI in education," "standardized testing," "homeschool,"
"online learning," "test anxiety," "college admissions."

## 2. Guest post pitch — parenting/education blogs

Target mid-tier parenting and homeschool blogs that publicly accept guest contributions
(check their site for a "Write for us" page first). Pitch the idea, not a finished draft —
most editors want to shape angle/length themselves.

```
Subject: Guest post idea: [working title]

Hi [editor name],

I write for TutorA, an online 1:1 tutoring marketplace, and I'd like to pitch a guest
post for [blog name] — no promotional angle, just a practical piece your readers can
actually use:

"[Working title, e.g. '5 Signs Your Child Might Need a Tutor (and 3 Signs They Don't)']"

[2-3 sentence outline of what the piece actually covers — real, useful content, not a
disguised ad. E.g.: "I'd cover the practical signals parents can watch for at home —
falling grades vs. falling confidence, homework time creeping up, avoidance behavior —
and when it's actually a teaching-style mismatch rather than a knowledge gap."]

Happy to write it on spec if that's easier, or adjust the angle to whatever's worked
well for your readers before. One contextual link back to tutora.it.com in an author
bio would be appreciated, but I'm flexible on placement.

Thanks for considering it,
[Your name]
tutora.support@gmail.com
```

**Real angle ideas that link naturally to specific pages** (use the actual page as the
link target, not the homepage — more relevant anchor context):
- "How to tell if your child needs Algebra I help or a fresh teaching approach" → link to `/subjects/algebra-i`
- "AP Physics 1 vs AP Physics C: which one is your teenager actually taking?" → link to `/subjects/ap-physics` (the chooser page — genuinely useful to link here, it's built to answer exactly this)
- "What a 'personally matched' tutor actually means (and why it's different from browsing profiles)" → link to `/guarantee`
- "Is online tutoring actually effective for [subject]?" → link to the relevant `/courses/[slug]` or `/subjects/[slug]` page for that subject

## 5. Reddit / Quora — real answers, not link drops

Do not post a link in the first reply to a thread — most subreddits remove this
immediately and it reads as spam. Answer the actual question first; only mention TutorA
if it's genuinely relevant to your answer, and expect most of these links to be
nofollow (that's fine — the value here is referral traffic and brand-mention signal,
not direct link equity).

Relevant communities: r/HomeworkHelp, r/tutoring, r/Parenting, r/homeschool, plus Quora
topics like "online tutoring," "test prep," "homeschooling."

---

## What to avoid (carried over from the original audit)

Do not purchase links, use automated directory-submission services, or participate in
link-exchange schemes. Do not send the school/PTA email with an offer you won't honor.
Do not invent stats, founding dates, or credentials in any pitch — every fillable
bracket above should be filled with something real or left more general, never fabricated.
